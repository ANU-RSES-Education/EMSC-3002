# Ductile Deformation

Ductile deformation occurs when rocks accommodate large deformations without fracturing.
This module covers how to recognise the elements of ductile deformation — folding, shearing
and stretching — and how to read them for the kinematic and paleostress information they
carry.

We look first at the geometry of folds, then at the mechanisms at work when rock layers
fold, then at the structures associated with folding (cleavages, lineations, boudinage),
and finally at shear zones and their kinematic indicators.

## What you will learn in this module

At the end of this module you will be able to:

- **classify folds** according to fold terminology
- understand **folding mechanisms**
- find **second order** folds and cleavage
- identify **folding and faulting in extensional regimes**
- identify **folding and faulting in compressional regimes**
- recognise the structures associated with folding, and use them to retrieve
  kinematic and paleostress information
- recognise **shear zones** and their kinematic indicators
