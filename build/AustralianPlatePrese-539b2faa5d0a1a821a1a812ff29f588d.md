# Presentation: Structure & Evolution of the Australian Plate

The goal of this presentation is to give you the opportunity to apply some concepts from the course to understand the structure and evolution of the Australian Plate.

Select a topic related to the structure and evolution of the Australian Plate, building on the course lectures and the research-level talks given during the semester. You then deliver a short presentation (8 minutes including questions) and provide a short written summary that applies course concepts while incorporating additional background research. The goal is to communicate something interesting to your peers that demonstrates understanding of both the theoretical concepts and their application to the Australian geological context.

::::{admonition} What to hand in

 - **The slide deck.** Aim for about 5 slides: the talk has to fit an 8-minute slot, including questions.
 - **A written summary** of about 500 words. This can be the script of your presentation with annotation, or presenter notes in the slides. It lets you add references and explanations that would otherwise spoil the flow of your talk. There are no penalties for going longer, but no advantages either.

::::

## Guidelines for the talk

 - Clearly identify the topic and how it relates to an aspect of the course.
 - Reference all figures and cite all data. On the slide a simple name / year citation is enough; give the full references after the final slide or in the presenter notes.
 - We assess your ability to present the topic and convey information. This is not about presentation style: it is about whether you can teach a complicated topic in a simple way.
 - You should be able to answer simple questions from your peers, to show that you have background knowledge of the topic.

## Learning outcomes assessed

These are outcomes 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the [course learning outcomes](../Introductory_Remarks.md):

 1. Describe the broad geological history of the Australian plate and place its major structures in their tectonic context.
 2. Explain the relationship between plate kinematics, plate tectonics forces and the deformation patterns in the continental crust.
 4. Predict the geometry and location of structures at depth or in areas of poor outcrop through the use of geological and geophysical observations.
 5. Interpret the relative timing of formation of structures, the kinematics of deformation, and progressive deformation histories in geological settings and laboratory / computer analogues.
