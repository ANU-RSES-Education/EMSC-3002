## Module I: Global Tectonics (Questions 1-4)

### Question 1: Earth Structure
The lithosphere is best described as:

A. The crust only
B. The crust plus the uppermost bear-rigid mantle
C. The coolest part of the mantle where elastic stresses are large but excluding the weak continental crust.
D. The asthenosphere plus crust
E. Only the oceanic crust

**Answer: B**

**Diagram suggestion**: Cross-section of Earth's layers showing crust, lithosphere, asthenosphere, and mantle with approximate depths (0-100 km for oceanic lithosphere, 0-200 km for continental lithosphere).

---

### Question 2: Plate Motions and Triple Junctions
Which of the following triple junction configurations is always stable?

A. Transform-Transform-Transform (TTT)
B. Ridge-Ridge-Ridge (RRR)
C. Trench-Trench-Trench (FFF)
D. All triple junctions are stable
E. No triple junctions are stable

**Answer: B**

---

### Question 3: Subduction Zone Complexity
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** In the diagram showing a subduction zone cross-section, normal faulting occurs at the outer rise because:

A. The subducting plate is under compression
B. The subducting plate is being bent, creating extensional stresses on the upper surface
C. The overriding plate is pulling the subducting plate down
D. Magma is forcing the plate apart
E. Slab rollback causes extension

**Answer: B**

**Diagram needed**: Cross-section of subduction zone showing: trench, outer rise with normal faults, bending oceanic plate, megathrust interface, and upper plate. Should clearly show the curvature of the subducting slab and the location of outer rise normal faults.

---

### Question 4: Continental vs. Oceanic Deformation
The Himalayan-Tibetan system demonstrates which of the following? (Select all that apply)

A. Continental crust can subduct like oceanic crust
B. Continental deformation is diffuse rather than localized
C. Extension can occur in Tibet despite ongoing convergence due to gravitational collapse
D. Escape tectonics with conjugate strike-slip faults
E. All convergent boundaries produce the same structures

**Answer: B, C, and D**

## Module II: Structural Geology (Questions 5-8)

### Question 5: Tectonic Stress Regimes
In a normal faulting regime, the principal stress orientations are:

A. σ₁ horizontal E-W, σ₂ horizontal N-S, σ₃ vertical
B. σ₁ vertical, σ₂ horizontal, σ₃ horizontal
C. σ₁ horizontal, σ₂ vertical, σ₃ horizontal
D. All principal stresses are horizontal
E. All principal stresses are equal

**Answer: B**

### Question 6: Thrust Fault Geometry
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** The diagram shows a thrust fault with ramp-flat geometry. Flats form preferentially:

A. In strong, competent layers to maximize displacement
B. In weak layers such as shale or salt
C. Only at the surface
D. Perpendicular to bedding
E. At random locations in the rock sequence

**Answer: B**

**Diagram needed**: Cross-section showing thrust fault with clear ramp-flat geometry. Should show: weak layer (shale/salt) hosting flat, ramp cutting through competent layers (sandstone/limestone), multiple flats and ramps, décollement surface, and hanging wall/footwall labels.

### Question 7: Extensional Structures
Which statement best distinguishes pure shear from simple shear rifting models?

A. Pure shear produces symmetric rifts; simple shear produces asymmetric half-graben
B. Simple shear produces symmetric rifts; pure shear produces asymmetric rifts
C. Both produce identical rift geometries
D. Pure shear only occurs in oceanic crust
E. Simple shear requires no faulting

**Answer: A**

### Question 8: Strike-Slip Structures
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** At a releasing bend on a strike-slip fault (shown in the diagram), you would expect to find:

A. Pop-up structures and local compression
B. Pull-apart basins and local extension
C. No deformation
D. Thrust faults only
E. (A and D)

**Answer: B**

**Diagram needed**: Map view of a dextral (right-lateral) strike-slip fault with both a releasing bend (stepover to the right creating a pull-apart basin) and a restraining bend (stepover to the left creating a pop-up). Label both features clearly.

## Module III: Stress, Strain, and Rheology (Questions 9-12)

### Question 9: Stress vs. Strain
Which statement correctly distinguishes stress from strain?

A. Stress is the deformation; strain is the force
B. Stress is force per unit area (cause); strain is deformation (effect)
C. Stress and strain are identical
D. Stress only occurs in ductile deformation; strain only in brittle
E. Strain always equals stress divided by time

**Answer: B**

### Question 10: Anderson's Theory of Faulting
According to Anderson's theory, faults form at approximately what angle to σ₁?

A. 0° (parallel)
B. 15°
C. 30°
D. 60°
E. 90° (perpendicular)

**Answer: C**

### Question 11: Mohr Circle and Failure
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** On the Mohr circle diagram shown, the failure envelope represents:

A. The elastic limit of the rock
B. The combinations of normal stress and shear stress that will cause failure
C. The principal stress orientations
D. The strain rate in the rock
E. The temperature at which melting occurs

**Answer: B**

**Diagram needed**: Mohr circle showing: horizontal axis (σₙ - normal stress), vertical axis (τ - shear stress), circle representing stress states, σ₁ and σ₃ marked on horizontal axis, failure envelope (Mohr-Coulomb criterion) as straight line tangent to circle, angle φ (friction angle), and C (cohesion) intercept.

### Question 12: Brittle-Ductile Transition
The brittle-ductile transition in the Earth's crust:

A. Occurs at a fixed depth of exactly 15 km everywhere
B. Depends on temperature, pressure, strain rate, and rock type
C. Only occurs in oceanic crust
D. Separates the crust from the mantle
E. Does not exist in continental crust

**Answer: B**

## Module IV: Brittle Deformation (Questions 13-16)

### Question 13: Byerlee's Law
Byerlee's Law demonstrates that:

A. Friction coefficient varies greatly between different rock types
B. Friction coefficient is remarkably constant (μ ≈ 0.6-0.85) for most rocks
C. Friction increases with temperature
D. There is no relationship between normal stress and shear stress
E. Only granites follow this relationship

**Answer: B**

### Question 14: Pore Fluid Pressure
The effective stress law (σ'ₙ = σₙ - Pf) explains:

A. Why high pore fluid pressure strengthens rocks
B. Why high pore fluid pressure can trigger failure by reducing effective normal stress
C. Why fluids have no effect on rock strength
D. Only applies to metamorphic rocks
E. (B and D)

**Answer: B**

### Question 15: Joint Characteristics
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** Plumose structures on a joint surface indicate:

A. The direction of shear displacement
B. The direction of joint propagation
C. The age of the rock
D. The temperature during formation
E. Nothing useful for interpretation

**Answer: B**

**Diagram needed**: Photograph or diagram of joint surface showing plumose structure with labeled features: plume axis (origin point), hackle marks radiating outward, arrest lines (concentric pauses), and arrow showing propagation direction away from origin.

### Question 16: Earthquake Focal Mechanisms
**[DIAGRAM QUESTION]** In a focal mechanism (beach ball) diagram, the black and white quadrants represent:

A. Black = compressional first motions; White = tensional first motions
B. Black = old earthquakes; White = new earthquakes
C. Black = deep earthquakes; White = shallow earthquakes
D. Black = magnitude; White = intensity
E. They are randomly assigned colors with no meaning

**Answer: A**

**Diagram needed**: Three focal mechanism examples showing: (1) Normal fault (tensional axis vertical), (2) Thrust fault (compressional axis vertical), (3) Strike-slip fault (both axes horizontal). Each should show the black/white quadrant pattern clearly and label P-axis (compressional) and T-axis (tensional).

### Question 20: Shear Sense Indicators
