## H<sub>2</sub> Exploration
- Sources uncertain /diverse
- Three main types of source rocks have been identified:
- Ultrabasic rocks
- Iron rich cratons
- Uranium rich rocks.
- (1 & 2) the production of H<sub>2</sub> is linked to the oxidation of Fe(II) by H<sub>2</sub>O
- (3) H<sub>2</sub> production from the radiolysis of H<sub>2</sub>O by natural radioactivity
- Gaucher, E. C. (2020). New Perspectives in the Industrial Exploration for Native Hydrogen. Elements, 16(1), 8–9. https://doi.org/10.2138/gselements.16.1.8

<div class="cols">
<div class="wide">

When they are deforming (long-term)
Faults slip if the forces acting in the shear direction are large enough compared to the forces acting to hold them together (friction)
Stress field and also orientation of the faults dictate this.
Fault movements change the stress conditions felt by other faults.

## Stress & Flow Models
<div class="cols">
<div class="wide">

Model description

Stress model: apply boundary velocity in orientation given by stress map.  Top is free to move. 200k - 400k 2nd  order simplex elements.
Faults are weak shear-zones defined by distance to an embedded surface. Each is a vertical curtain.
Flow models: Uniform source at the base, permeability by voting model: low from near-surface MT, high from near-surface MT, low from fault deformation, high from fault deformation.

## Topological optimisation
Exploring Uncertainty

- Forward model (predict flow / stress etc) — here is a deformation model:
- $$\nabla ⋅\left[ \eta \left[ \nabla v+( \nabla v{)}^{T} \right] \right]- \nabla p+\beta (x)=0$$
- Where $\beta (x)$ is some sort of “design parameter” that controls the shape that we are going to vary. We then can solve a related problem
- $$\nabla ⋅\left[ \eta \left[ \nabla u+( \nabla u{)}^{T} \right] \right]- \nabla q+\beta (x)= \partial J/ \partial v$$
- $J$ is the optimisation “cost function” a.k.a. the misfit to the data e.g.
- $J=(v-{v} _ {0})⋅(v-{v} _ {0})$

## Topological optimisation
Exploring Uncertainty

Surface expression

Uncertainty at depth

Once we solve $v$ and $u$, we can compute things like
$\partial J/ \partial \beta$
which tells us how to adjust the design of the model ($\beta$) so that we improve the misfit to the data while moving from one valid solution of the model equations to another.
In topological optimisation, we combine $v$ and $u$ to tell us how to move the boundary of the shape described by $\beta$.
We can also see where the model does not vary when we change $\beta$ which is probably the most useful thing for us — in this we need to find other data which is sensitive to the change
